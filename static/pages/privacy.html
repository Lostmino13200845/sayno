The full policy

What the extension does on your device

For every web page you open, the extension checks the address locally: it looks for warning signs in the link (look-alike names, disguised addresses, risky endings and similar) and compares a hash of the address with a list of known-bad fingerprints stored on your device. If the page asks for card details, the extension decides locally whether to block it. When you choose "Check this text with SAYNO" or paste something into the popup, the text is analysed by the same rules, on your device. Card numbers you type are never read out of the page.

What your device downloads

The block lists are public files on GitHub Pages. Your device downloads them in the background, about every 15 minutes, and nothing is uploaded. Only when a page matches the local list, it also fetches one small file named by a single hash byte, which does not reveal the page. The host can see that your device asked for these files, but nothing about the pages you visit or the messages you check.

GitHub, which hosts the files, keeps standard web-server logs under its own privacy statement. SAYNO does not receive or keep them. This website uses fonts that are stored with the site, so your browser does not contact a font service either.

The web scanner on this website

The message and website checks, and the practice tutorial, run entirely in your browser tab. What you paste is not uploaded. The one exception is the password check on the Protect yourself page: your browser hashes the password itself and sends only the first 5 characters of the hash to Have I Been Pwned (api.pwnedpasswords.com), which answers with all hashes sharing that start; your browser does the final comparison. The password never leaves your device.

Reporting a scam from this website (optional)

The Report a scam page lets you help get a scam blocked. Nothing is sent unless you press Send report after ticking the consent box. If you do, the blanks you filled in (the link, phone number or sender address, any message text, the options you ticked and your notes) are sent to a small relay run by the SAYNO team, which emails them from the SAYNO team address. Links and scam emails are emailed to anti-phishing organisations (currently the Anti-Phishing Working Group) so they can be added to block lists; every report is also copied to the SAYNO team mailbox, where we may keep it to spot trends and avoid duplicates. We can delete a report on request.

Your name and email address are never part of the report, and the relay does not receive your IP address. Please do not type card numbers, passwords or codes, or anything about yourself. Because the report is sent through Google services, Google handles the transmission under its own policies. You can instead copy the report or open it in your own email app, which sends it from your address. The browser extension never sends reports or anything you check; this applies only to the website page you choose to use.

What is stored on your device

Your settings (for example your chosen bank), the block-list fingerprints, and the rating of each open tab for the current browser session. The website remembers your checklist ticks in your browser. You can remove all of it by uninstalling the extension or clearing site data.

What we do not collect

  • ×Browsing history, or a list of the sites you visit.
  • ×Page contents, text you check, form entries, passwords or card numbers.
  • ×Your name, email address or any account (there are no accounts).

If you use the "Stay informed" box in the footer, or the "Report a problem" form, your email app opens with a message to us. We only receive it if you send it. Reports sent from the "Report a scam" page are described above.

Where the block lists come from

Public threat feeds: URLhaus and ThreatFox (abuse.ch), OpenPhish, Phishing Army, CERT Polska, the Phishing.Database project and a list of disposable email providers. They are fetched and republished as hashes about every 15 minutes by an automated GitHub workflow.

What the extension needs access to

Chrome shows a standard list of permissions when you add any extension. SAYNO asks only for what its protection needs, and everything it sees is handled on your device.

  • It looks at the page address and which fields a page asks for, so it can warn you before you type card details.
  • It remembers your settings and keeps its public block list up to date.
  • It adds a right-click option so you can check a piece of text or a link.
  • Notifications are optional and stay off until you switch them on.

It never sends the pages you visit, what you type or the messages you check anywhere, and it does not ask for your passwords, browsing history, cookies or bookmarks.
