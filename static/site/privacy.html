Privacy policy

Applies to the SAYNO browser extension and the SAYNO website (including the web scanner). Last updated 4 October 2026.

The short version

  • Everything is checked on your device. The pages you visit, the text you select or paste, and the card numbers you type are never sent to SAYNO or to anyone else.
  • SAYNO has no server that receives your data and no accounts. It publishes plain files (the block lists) that your device downloads.
  • We do not sell data, show ads or build profiles.

What the extension does on your device

For every web page you open, the extension checks the address locally: it looks for warning signs in the link (look-alike names, disguised addresses, risky endings and similar) and compares a hash of the address with a list of known-bad fingerprints stored on your device. If the page asks for card details, the extension decides locally whether to block it. When you choose "Check this text with SAYNO" or paste something into the popup, the text is analysed by the same rules, on your device. Card numbers you type are never read out of the page.

What your device downloads, and what the file host can see

The block lists are static files on GitHub Pages. Your device downloads them; nothing is uploaded.

WhatWhenWhat the host can learn
meta.json, a few hundred bytesAbout every 30 minutes, and at start-upThat your IP address asked for the file, and when. It reveals nothing about what you browse.
prefixes.bin, about 2.4 MBOnly when the published list changed (a few times a day at most)The same as above.
One small "shard" file, named by the first byte of a hash (256 possible files)Rarely: only when a page matches the local listThat a shard such as 3f was fetched. A hash byte cannot be turned back into a page address, and many thousands of different pages share each shard.

GitHub, which hosts the files, keeps standard web-server logs under its own privacy statement. SAYNO does not receive or keep them.

The web scanner on this website

The scanner at /app and the practice tutorial run entirely in your browser tab. What you paste is not uploaded. The one exception is the password check: your browser hashes the password itself and sends only the first 5 characters of the hash to Have I Been Pwned (api.pwnedpasswords.com), which answers with all hashes sharing that start; your browser does the final comparison. The password never leaves your device.

What is stored on your device

Your settings (for example your chosen bank), the block-list fingerprints, and the rating of each open tab for the current browser session. The website remembers your checklist ticks in your browser. You can remove all of it by uninstalling the extension or clearing site data.

What we do not collect

  • Browsing history, or a list of the sites you visit.
  • Page contents, text you check, form entries, passwords or card numbers.
  • Your name, email address or any account (there are no accounts).

Where the block lists come from

Public threat feeds: URLhaus and ThreatFox (abuse.ch), OpenPhish, Phishing Army, CERT Polska, the Phishing.Database project and a list of disposable email providers. They are fetched and republished as hashes every few hours by an automated GitHub workflow.
